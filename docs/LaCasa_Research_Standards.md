# LaCasa research standards

Version 3.0 · 22 October 2026 · Owner: K
Follow this file for every LaCasa research request, in ChatGPT or Claude. It replaces the long prompt K used to paste each time.

## 1. The four commands

K types one short line. Do the matching job below without asking K to approve a plan. Ask at most three questions together, and only when the answer would substantially change the recommendation; otherwise state a reasonable assumption and carry on.

| K types | You do | Ends with |
|---|---|---|
| `Find: <item>, budget S$<amount>` (+ optional notes) | Stage 1: build the real shop inventory, screen it, and recommend a shortlist of 2–3 models | A ready-to-copy line: `Deep dive: A, B, C` |
| `Deep dive: <model>, <model>, <model>` | Stage 2: full specs and functions, word of mouth, side-by-side comparison, decision | The WEBSITE CARD |
| `Quick: <item or models>, budget S$<amount>` | Both stages, lighter, for small or low-risk items (vacuums, kettles, cleaning service) | The WEBSITE CARD |
| `Recheck: <models>` | Current price and stock at eligible sellers, plus sale dates | An updated WEBSITE CARD (prices, where to buy, buy timing only) |

Item names are plain words ("fridge", "living room lights"). Never ask K for codes or IDs.

## 3. Buying priorities

1. **Within budget.** Every option must fit the budget K gives. If nothing good fits, say so plainly and show the cheapest option you would buy confidently, labelled "over budget by S$X". Never stretch the budget silently.
2. **Reliability and build quality** come first, then repairability and responsive local after-sales support.
3. **Value for money.** Performance matters, but don't pay a large premium for small improvements K will barely notice.
4. **Real, branded products.** Prefer established manufacturers with genuine engineering expertise, a long record in this category, real control over quality and dependable Singapore support. Avoid anonymous white-label or rebadged brands and sellers with unclear accountability.
5. **Word of mouth.** What real owners say over months of use outweighs launch reviews and marketing.
6. Find underrated options when the evidence supports them; never force a hidden gem.

**Who really makes it.** Investigate who owns the brand, who designs and manufactures the product, and who handles the Singapore warranty. Distinguish an actual manufacturer, a brand using contract manufacturing, a licensed brand name and a generic rebadger. Don't treat "OEM" as a synonym for poor quality, assume a famous brand makes everything itself, or invent factory ownership. State what is verified and what is unknown.

## 4. Availability first (real stock only)

Build every recommendation from what K can actually buy now. A globally praised product that isn't sold here is useless.

**Eligible sellers**
- Singapore: Gain City, Harvey Norman SG, COURTS, Best Denki, Audio House, Mega Discount Store, Parisilk, official brand stores, verified official Shopee SG stores. Category specialists with verifiable SG premises and warranty are fine (lighting and fan shops, mattress showrooms, bathroom suppliers).
- Malaysia/JB, where worthwhile: Harvey Norman MY, Senheng/senQ, TBM, verified authorised JB retailers, verified official Shopee MY stores.
- Amazon only when the selected offer's actual seller is the brand or an authorised distributor. A brand Store page or "fulfilled by Amazon" alone doesn't prove that.
- Never: Lazada, anonymous shops, used or refurbished items, undisclosed parallel imports. A name containing "official", or a marketplace badge, isn't proof on its own.

**How to build the inventory (Find and Quick)**
1. List the relevant category pages at the eligible sellers. Go through every accessible page, load-more and relevant filter (size, capacity), not just page one or sponsored results.
2. Record every model, then deduplicate exact regional model, size and revision. Keep separate seller offers.
3. Screen each unique model against K's needs, size, budget, stock and support. Give a brief exclusion reason, so a cheaper or less famous contender is never silently dropped.
4. Show a compact coverage table: shop/category URL · checked (SGT) · pages/listings examined · unique models · complete? · gaps.

Never claim complete coverage or the market's absolute best price without it. Respect access controls (section 6).

**Stock**
- Verify stock on the exact product page, with the right variant and seller selected.
- Label it: ready stock · low stock · confirmed branch stock · preorder/backorder · out of stock · discontinued · unverified. Add the source and SGT time.
- A search snippet, price, past sales count or add-to-cart button doesn't prove stock.
- Only ready or low stock can be recommended. Others stay outside the rankings. A discontinued item may appear only as a clearly labelled clearance option with verified new stock and warranty.
- Recheck the winners' stock and payable price at the end. Give an in-stock fallback for any low-stock winner.
- Never order anything or contact sellers.

## 5. How to judge evidence

- State the research date in Singapore time. Prioritise current and previous model years and evidence from the last 12–24 months, and check older owner reports for long-term patterns.
- **Facts:** official regional product pages, manuals, spec sheets, warranty terms, service information and recall notices.
- **Performance:** independent tests with transparent methods (e.g., RTINGS, HDTVTest, Consumer Reports, Which?, CHOICE, credible category specialists). Check access, funding, sponsorship, affiliate links and methods; never call a whole publication "unbiased".
- **Durability and support:** first-hand owners, long-term updates and documented repairs.
- **Actively look for failures:** recurring defects, returns, warranty denials, repair delays, unavailable parts and unresponsive support. Also look for fixes, firmware changes or revised models that genuinely solved an issue.
- **Weigh complaints carefully:** separate minor annoyances, isolated failures, recurring model defects, seller problems and wider brand or service problems.
  - A credible repeated pattern of major failures lowers the recommendation, or rules out the brand when the evidence is that wide.
  - Never infer failure rates from raw complaint counts.
  - Few complaints about a new or obscure product don't prove reliability, and a 2026 model can't have years of proven durability.
- **Exact regional model:** check the model code, size, revision and warranty provider. Don't assume US, EU, SG and MY versions are identical.
- **Open the sources:** read decisive sources, not just snippets. Never invent prices, quotes, reviews, consensus or content you couldn't open.
- **Confidence labels:** strong, moderate or limited evidence, each with a short reason. Keep verified facts, owner reports and your own judgement visibly separate.

## 6. Word of mouth: communities, videos and comments (required)

**Where to look**
- Reddit: r/askSingapore, r/singapore, r/malaysia and product-specific subreddits, including their wikis, pinned guides and megathreads (check update dates).
- HardwareZone forums and Lowyat forums.
- Public Facebook owner groups and posts.
- YouTube reviews and long-term updates.
- Shopee/retailer reviews from verified buyers.
- Xiaohongshu where relevant to the brand.

**Read the comments, not just the posts.** For each key thread or video, read top, recent, critical and dissenting comments and the replies under them, and follow later ownership updates. If there are too many, say which mix you read. Look for:
- owners confirming or challenging a review
- failures after longer use and warranty experiences
- regional differences, corrections and pinned creator replies

**Videos:** watch the relevant sections when you can. Otherwise use the transcript, description and linked data, and say exactly what you accessed. Never claim you watched a video when you only read its transcript or title. Record the channel, date, exact model and useful timestamps.

**Agreement:** treat repeated recommendations as possible consensus, not truth. Establish whether agreement comes from unrelated first-hand owners across threads and dates, or from one guide or reviewer repeated. A video, its reposts and comments repeating it are one source, not several. Call agreement strong, mixed or limited, and include meaningful minority objections.

**Spam and shills:** check for bots, undisclosed ads, affiliate spam and coordinated promotion, including community call-outs and moderator warnings. Unsupported accusations stay unverified; enthusiasm or a new account alone proves nothing.

**Access:**
- If you have a browser tool signed in as K (such as Claude in Chrome or ChatGPT's agent browser), use it to read Reddit, HardwareZone, Lowyat, Facebook groups K can see, YouTube comments and Shopee reviews at a normal reading pace.
- Otherwise, never bypass logins, paywalls or blocks. Name the gap and use other accessible discussions.
- Cross-check every community or video favourite against the exact SG/MY variant, current authorised stock and local warranty. An overseas favourite that isn't sold here can't enter the rankings; name the closest local alternative without assuming it's identical.

## 7. Prices, Singapore vs JB, and sales

**Offers**
- For finalists, record at least two eligible sellers where practical: exact model, seller, direct link, date checked, stock, warranty provider and actual payable price.
- Keep unconditional prices separate from bank-card deals, memberships, trade-ins, limited vouchers, cashback, coins and store credit.
- Include compulsory accessories, delivery, installation and disposal of the old unit. Future credits are not cash discounts.

**JB**
- Show MYR and SGD at a dated exchange rate.
- Count the full cost of bringing the item into Singapore: applicable taxes, transport, installation, compatibility (plugs, voltage, Singapore safety marks for electrical items) and warranty or repair arrangements.
- Never assume a Malaysian warranty is valid in Singapore or that a JB shop delivers across the border.
- Say whether the real saving justifies the hassle and support risk.

**Sales calendar** (from today to the item's deadline, or the next 90 days)
- 11.11 (11 Nov), Black Friday (27 Nov), 12.12 (12 Dec), payday sales, brand days, retailer anniversaries, warehouse sales and verified official livestreams on Shopee/TikTok.
- Say which eligible shops actually join for this category. Mark each event confirmed or expected; never present last year's deal as this year's.
- Compare final payable prices after valid stackable discounts. Mark new-user, limited-redemption and livestream-only offers as conditional.
- Compare against recent actual selling prices, not inflated RRPs.
- Give a buy-now or wait call, a justified target price, the next useful sale date and what to verify before paying.

## 8. Stage 1 — FIND (output)

Plain English, short paragraphs, tables where they help.

1. **Quick answer.** Provisional best fit, best value, budget pick and performance pick, each with seller, price, stock and one reason. Flag anything that would change the answer.
2. **Understand the product.** Types and technologies in a table (type · what it means · strengths · weaknesses · who should choose it), what's genuinely new versus marketing, the specs that matter for K, sensible minimums, worthwhile upgrades, extras to ignore and showroom tests.
3. **Brands.** Up to five suitable brands: category background · historical vs recent reputation · reliability evidence and limits · SG/MY service and warranty · strengths · concerns · who it suits. Give a preference order for K, and say what K loses with the cheaper brand. Never invent a reliability league table.
4. **Inventory coverage table** (section 4), plus excluded models with one-line reasons.
5. **Models by tier.** Set Budget, Mid and Splurge boundaries from current local prices for K's size and budget, and explain them. Rank up to five models per tier that pass availability: model · price, seller, stock and time · strengths · drawbacks · reliability evidence and confidence · best for. Fewer is fine.
6. **Early red flags.** Known defects, recalls or support problems for the leading models.
7. **Shortlist.** The 2–3 models you would deep-dive and why, ideally covering a cheaper safe choice and a stronger upgrade within budget. End with one copyable line: `Deep dive: <model A>, <model B>, <model C>`.

## 9. Stage 2 — DEEP DIVE (output)

For the 2–3 models K names. Re-verify each exact model, variant and stock first.

1. **Full details and functions.** From official specs and manuals: dimensions including required clearances and door swing, capacity, every mode and function, app or smart features, noise, energy and water ratings with yearly running cost, consumables and their prices, installation needs, warranty terms and length, service network, and what's in the box.
2. **Side-by-side comparison** in grouped rows (e.g., Size & fit · Performance · Features · Running costs · Warranty & support). Use "Not stated" where the maker doesn't say; never guess.
3. **What owners say.** A table for each model: sources consulted · recurring praise · recurring complaints and key replies · consensus · access limits. Link the important comments and timestamped video sections.
4. **Reliability and what to avoid.** Repeated failures, their severity and scope, whether they're fixed, and credible criticisms of your own top pick, with confidence labels.
5. **Where to buy.** Singapore vs JB per section 7, with at least two eligible sellers per model where possible.
6. **Is spending more worth it?** The cheapest option K can buy confidently, the sweet spot, and where diminishing returns begin. Make it concrete: "Pay S$X more for B instead of A to get …; worth it / not worth it for LaCasa." Include running costs over a stated period with stated assumptions.
7. **Final decision.** What you would buy and why, the best alternative and what would change your mind, what K gives up by spending less, buy now or wait, the target price and a fallback if stock runs out.
8. **Showroom and before-payment checklist:** fit, practical tests, exact model, seller authorisation, warranty exclusions and the return/defect policy.
9. **Sources,** grouped: official specs and warranty · independent tests and videos · communities and owner comments · shop offers and campaigns.
10. **The WEBSITE CARD** (section 11).

## 10. The quick take (the part the family reads first)

Most of the family will read only this, so it must make the choice obvious in ten seconds and be completely true.

- **10-second answer** (per item): one or two sentences, e.g., "Pick Beta: quietest and S$90 a year cheaper to run. Choose Gamma only if the niche measures at least 72 cm."
- **Per option:**
  - *Headline:* 8 words or fewer, the one thing that wins hearts, specific and true. "Quietest here, you won't hear it from the sofa" beats "Amazing fridge".
  - *Why you'll love it:* one line, the biggest everyday benefit.
  - *Watch out:* one line, the most important drawback or risk.
  - *Best for:* one line.
  - *3 pros and 3 cons,* most important first.
- Every claim must come from the evidence in the report. Use numbers where they help (dB, S$/year, cm), no hype words, no exaggeration. If owners report a serious problem, it goes in "Watch out" even if the product is the pick.

## 11. The WEBSITE CARD (exact format)

End every Deep dive, Quick and Recheck with this block inside ONE code block, so K can copy it in one tap. Plain words only, no codes. Fill every line; write "Not stated" or "None" when empty.

SALES WORTH WAITING FOR: <11.11, Black Friday, 12.12, or None>

OPTION A
Name: <brand + exact model>

For a "look" decision (e.g., living room lighting), each option is a scenario ("Cove + fan"). Its Name describes the scenario, and the compare rows say what it includes.
